Step 1: Understand the structure of the table. The table contains data on various engine types and their performance metrics under different scenarios.  
Step 2: Identify and describe each column:
- 'engine type': Type of engine (e.g., rocket, turbojet, turbofan).
- 'scenario': Operating condition (e.g., vacuum, Mach 1, cruise).
- 'sfc in lb/(lbf h)': Specific fuel consumption in imperial units.
- 'sfc in g/(kn s)': Specific fuel consumption in metric units.
- 'specific impulse (s)': Efficiency measure of propulsion, higher is better.
- 'effective exhaust velocity (m/s)': Velocity at which exhaust gases are expelled, directly related to efficiency.

Step 3: Derive notable insights:
- The Rolls-Royce/SNECMA Olympus 593 engine (used in Concorde) has the highest specific impulse and exhaust velocity, indicating superior efficiency at Mach 2.
- The J-58 turbojet (used in SR-71) has a very high effective exhaust velocity (18,587 m/s), reflecting its performance at high speeds.
- Rocket engines like NK-33 and SSME have lower specific fuel consumption in vacuum but high specific impulse, suitable for space applications.
- Turbofan engines (like CF6-80C2B1F) have low SFC values, indicating fuel efficiency at subsonic cruise speeds (e.g., Boeing 747).
