Step 1: Understand the structure and content of the table. The table contains data on a country's external debt and debt service over fiscal years from 1999 to 2018.  
Step 2: Identify and explain each column:
- "Fiscal Year": The year for which the data is reported.
- "Total External Debt in Million of US Dollars ($)": The total amount of external debt (debts owed to foreign creditors) in millions of USD.
- "Total Debt Service in Million of US Dollars ($)": The total amount of money required to service the debt (interest and principal payments) in millions of USD.
- "External Debt to GDP Ratio (%)": The ratio of external debt to the country's GDP, indicating the debt burden relative to economic size.
- "Debt Service Ratio (%)": The ratio of debt service to GDP, showing how much of the economy's output is used to pay off debt.

Step 3: Derive basic insights:
- The total external debt peaked in 2012 at $79,949 million and then slightly decreased, stabilizing around $73,000–$77,000 million by 2018.
- The external debt to GDP ratio declined significantly from 68.2% (2001) to 23.5% (2018), suggesting improved debt sustainability.
- The debt service ratio dropped from 17.1% (2002) to under 10% (from 2008 onward), indicating reduced financial pressure on the economy.
- From 2015 onward, the debt service ratio shows "-", indicating that the data is not available or not applicable for those years.
