Step 1: The question asks whether a higher crude birth rate causally influences the natural change in population.  
Step 2: Causal influence implies a direct, time-dependent relationship where changes in crude birth rate lead to changes in natural change. However, this requires more than just correlation — it requires causal inference, which is beyond the scope of simple descriptive statistics or correlation analysis from a table.  
Step 3: From the data, we can compute the correlation between crude birth rate and natural change to assess a possible linear relationship.  
Step 4: If there's a strong positive correlation, it might suggest a pattern, but correlation does not imply causation.  
Step 5: Therefore, we will calculate the correlation coefficient between "Crude birth rate (per 1000)" and "Natural change (per 1000)" and interpret it cautiously.

Note: Since the question asks about causality, and we cannot establish causation from observational data alone, we must state that while there may be a correlation, causality cannot be determined from this table.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Extract relevant columns
crude_birth_rate = df['Crude birth rate (per 1000)']
natural_change = df['Natural change (per 1000)']

# Calculate correlation
correlation = crude_birth_rate.corr(natural_change)

print(f"Final Answer: {correlation:.2f}")
```
