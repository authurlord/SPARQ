Step 1: The question asks for the total estimated exposure (mrem/hr) when combining two specimens, assuming that the calculated activity (Bq) is directly proportional to the specimen weight. This means we can scale the exposure based on the weight ratio.

Step 2: Identify the relevant rows:
- Specimen 1: 0.1 g / 4.08 mm → exposure = 0.0 mrem/hr
- Specimen 2: 10 g / 1.89 cm → exposure = 0.03 mrem/hr

Step 3: Since the exposure is assumed to be proportional to the weight, and the first specimen has a weight of 0.1 g and exposure 0.0, we can infer that the exposure value given (0.0) is likely due to rounding or negligible contribution.

Step 4: The second specimen (10 g) already has an exposure of 0.03 mrem/hr. The first specimen (0.1 g) is 1/100th of the second in weight, so its contribution would be 1/100th of the activity and thus 1/100th of the exposure.

Step 5: Calculate the exposure from the 0.1 g specimen:
- Exposure ∝ weight → (0.1 / 10) × 0.03 = 0.0003 mrem/hr

Step 6: Add both contributions:
- Total exposure = 0.0003 + 0.03 = 0.0303 mrem/hr

Step 7: Round to a reasonable precision and present.

```python
# Given data
weight_01g = 0.1
exposure_01g = 0.0

weight_10g = 10
exposure_10g = 0.03

# Proportional exposure for 0.1g specimen based on 10g specimen
proportional_exposure_01g = (weight_01g / weight_10g) * exposure_10g

# Total estimated exposure
```
