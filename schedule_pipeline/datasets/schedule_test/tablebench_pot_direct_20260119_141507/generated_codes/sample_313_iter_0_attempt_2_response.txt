Step 1: Identify the relevant rows where the category is "Best Actor" and the year is 2017 or earlier.  
Step 2: Filter the data to include only those entries with "Best Actor" and year ≤ 2017.  
Step 3: Among these, count the number of wins (where Result is "Won") for each film.  
Step 4: Determine which film has the highest number of wins.  

Note: The films are "Cock and Bull", "Dearest", and "Feather Flies to the Sky". However, "Dearest" was nominated in 2016 (not 2017 or earlier in terms of award year? Wait — 2016 is earlier than 2017). But the category "Best Supporting Actor" is not "Best Actor", so we exclude it. Only films in "Best Actor" category and year ≤ 2017 are considered.

From the table:
- 2017: "Cock and Bull" → Won at 24th Beijing College Student Film Festival and 8th China Film Director's Guild Awards → 2 wins
- 2017: "Feather Flies to the Sky" → Won at 23rd Shanghai Television Festival → 1 win
- 2016: "Dearest" → Nominated (not a win), and not in Best Actor → excluded

So only "Cock and Bull" and "Feather Flies to the Sky" are in Best Actor and ≤ 2017.

Thus, "Cock and Bull" has 2 wins, "Feather Flies to the Sky" has 1 win.

Therefore, "Cock and Bull" has the most award wins.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Filter for Best Actor category and year <= 2017
filtered_df = df[(df['Category'] == 'Best Actor') & (df['Year'].astype(int) <= 2017)]
# Group by 'Nominated work' and count wins (where Result == 'Won')
win_counts = filtered_df[filtered_df['Result'] == 'Won'].groupby('Nominated work').size()
# Find the film with the maximum wins
if not win_counts.empty:
    film_with_most_wins = win_counts.idxmax()
else:
    film_with_most_wins = None
print(f"Final Answer: {film_with_most_wins}")
```
