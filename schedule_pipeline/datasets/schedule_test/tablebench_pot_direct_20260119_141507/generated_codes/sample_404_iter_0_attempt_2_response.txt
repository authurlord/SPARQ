Step 1: Understand the structure of the table. The table contains data on different engine types and their performance metrics under various scenarios.  
Step 2: Describe each column:
- 'engine type': The type of engine (e.g., rocket, turbojet, turbofan).
- 'scenario': The operational condition (e.g., vacuum, Mach 1, cruise).
- 'sfc in lb/(lbf h)': Specific fuel consumption in imperial units.
- 'sfc in g/(kn s)': Specific fuel consumption in metric units.
- 'specific impulse (s)': A measure of propulsion efficiency, higher is better.
- 'effective exhaust velocity (m/s)': Indicates how fast the exhaust gases are expelled; higher values mean better efficiency.

Step 3: Identify notable insights:
- The Rolls-Royce/SNECMA Olympus 593 engine (used in Concorde) has the highest specific impulse (3012) and effective exhaust velocity (29553 m/s), indicating excellent efficiency at Mach 2.
- The J-58 turbojet (used in SR-71) has the highest effective exhaust velocity (18587 m/s), suggesting superior performance at high speeds.
- The CF6-80C2B1F turbofan has the highest specific impulse (5950) and effective exhaust velocity (58400 m/s), indicating it is optimized for efficient subsonic cruise (e.g., Boeing 747).
- Rocket engines like NK-33 and SSME have lower specific fuel consumption but high specific impulse in vacuum, which is typical for launch vehicles.

Step 4: Summarize the key trends: Performance peaks at high-speed or high-altitude conditions, with turbofans dominating in commercial aviation due to efficiency at cruise speeds.
