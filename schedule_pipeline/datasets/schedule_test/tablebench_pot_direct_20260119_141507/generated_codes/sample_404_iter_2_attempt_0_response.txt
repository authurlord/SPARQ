Step 1: Understand the structure of the table. The table contains data on different engine types and their performance metrics under various scenarios.  
Step 2: Identify and describe each column:
- "engine type": Type of engine (e.g., rocket, turbojet, turbofan).
- "scenario": Operating condition (e.g., vacuum, Mach 1, cruise).
- "sfc in lb/(lbf h)": Specific fuel consumption in imperial units.
- "sfc in g/(kn s)": Specific fuel consumption in metric units.
- "specific impulse (s)": Efficiency measure of propulsion, in seconds.
- "effective exhaust velocity (m/s)": Speed at which exhaust gases are expelled, directly related to efficiency.

Step 3: Derive insights:
- The "specific impulse" and "effective exhaust velocity" are inversely related to fuel consumption — higher values indicate better efficiency.
- The Ramjet and Concorde engines show very high specific impulse and exhaust velocity, indicating high efficiency at supersonic speeds.
- Turbofans (like CF6-80C2B1F) have the highest specific impulse and exhaust velocity, suggesting superior efficiency for commercial aircraft.
- Rocket engines (e.g., NK-33, SSME) perform well in vacuum conditions but have lower fuel efficiency in atmosphere (higher SFC).
