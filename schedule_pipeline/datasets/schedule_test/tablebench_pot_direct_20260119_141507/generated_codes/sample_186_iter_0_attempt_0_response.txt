Step 1: The user wants to understand the relationship between the number of candidates nominated and the percentage of popular vote over time.  
Step 2: We need to analyze whether there is a correlation or trend between these two variables across elections.  
Step 3: Load the dataframe and extract the relevant columns: "of candidates nominated" and "% of popular vote".  
Step 4: Convert the "of candidates nominated" column to numeric (it's currently a string with values like '60').  
Step 5: Convert the "% of popular vote" column to numeric (values like '0.21%').  
Step 6: Calculate the correlation coefficient between the two variables.  
Step 7: Print the correlation value to indicate the strength and direction of the relationship.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Convert the relevant columns to numeric, removing the % sign and handling formatting
df['of candidates nominated'] = pd.to_numeric(df['of candidates nominated'], errors='coerce')
df['% of popular vote'] = pd.to_numeric(df['% of popular vote'].str.rstrip('%'), errors='coerce')

# Calculate the correlation between the number of candidates nominated and percentage of popular vote
correlation = df['of candidates nominated'].corr(df['% of popular vote'])
print(f"Final Answer: {correlation:.3f}")
```
