Step 1: Understand the table structure and identify the main columns.  
The table contains data on polling results for political party support over time, with columns including polling firm, date of polling, link, and support percentages for three parties: Progressive Conservative, Liberal, and New Democratic.

Step 2: Identify key observations about trends in political party support.  
- The "Progressive Conservative" support starts at 71% in 2008 and increases to a peak of 82% in 2007 (earlier dates), then gradually decreases over time.  
- The "Liberal" support is generally lower than the Progressive Conservative and shows a slight upward trend from 12% in 2007 to 22% in 2009, but fluctuates.  
- The "New Democratic" support remains relatively stable, hovering around 8–25%, with a slight decline over time.  

Step 3: Provide a concise, descriptive summary based on these observations.

```python
import pandas as pd

# Load the data
df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Display the column names and a brief summary
print("Columns:", df.columns.tolist())
print("\nSample data:")
print(df.head())

# Analyze trends by converting 'date of polling' to a datetime format for time-based analysis
df['date_of_polling'] = pd.to_datetime(df['date of polling'], errors='coerce')

# Group by year and calculate average support for each party
df['year'] = df['date_of_polling'].dt.year
party_support_avg = df.groupby('year')[['progressive conservative', 'liberal', 'new democratic']].mean()

# Display the trend summary
print("\nAverage party support by year:")
print(party_support_avg)
```
