Step 1: The question asks which factor — population or per capita income — has a stronger causal relationship with median household income.  
Step 2: Since "causal relationship" is a complex concept, we interpret it here as which variable has a stronger correlation with median household income (as a proxy for causality in the absence of explicit causal data).  
Step 3: We compute the correlation between median household income and both population and per capita income.  
Step 4: Compare the absolute values of these correlations; the higher absolute value indicates a stronger relationship.  
Step 5: Output the factor (population or per capita income) with the stronger correlation.

Note: Per capita income is typically derived from total income divided by population, so it may have a weaker independent relationship with median household income compared to population.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert relevant columns to numeric
df['per capita income'] = pd.to_numeric(df['per capita income'], errors='coerce')
df['population'] = pd.to_numeric(df['population'], errors='coerce')
df['median household income'] = pd.to_numeric(df['median household income'], errors='coerce')

# Compute correlation with median household income
correlation_population = df['population'].corr(df['median household income'])
correlation_per_capita = df['per capita income'].corr(df['median household income'])

# Determine which has stronger correlation
if abs(correlation_population) > abs(correlation_per_capita):
    final_answer = "population"
else:
    final_answer = "per capita income"

print(f"Final Answer: {final_answer}")
```
