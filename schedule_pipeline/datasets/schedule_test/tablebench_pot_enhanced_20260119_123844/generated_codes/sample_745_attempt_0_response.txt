Step 3: To estimate the likely average finish, we calculate the average of 'avg finish' values across all years (1990–2002).  
Step 4: To estimate total winnings, we calculate the average of 'winnings' per race over the years, then multiply by 5 (the number of races in 2002).  
Step 5: We use the data from the 'avg finish' and 'winnings' columns, excluding any non-numeric or inconsistent entries.  
Step 6: Compute the average finish and projected winnings for 5 races.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Convert 'avg finish' to float
df['avg finish'] = pd.to_numeric(df['avg finish'], errors='coerce')
# Convert 'winnings' to float
df['winnings'] = pd.to_numeric(df['winnings'], errors='coerce')

# Calculate average finish across all seasons
avg_finish = df['avg finish'].mean()

# Calculate average winnings per race
avg_winnings_per_race = df['winnings'].mean() / df['starts'].mean()

# Projected winnings for 5 races (same as last active year)
projected_winnings = avg_winnings_per_race * 5
```
