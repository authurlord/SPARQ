We are asked whether an increase in eccentricity causes a corresponding increase in periselene or inclination. This is a question about correlation or trend between two variables.

Step-by-step approach:
1. Load the data into a DataFrame.
2. Extract the columns: 'eccentricity', 'periselene', and 'inclination'.
3. Check if there's a positive correlation between eccentricity and periselene, or between eccentricity and inclination.
4. We can compute the Pearson correlation coefficient for each pair to determine if there's a corresponding increase.

Note: The question is not asking for a direct causal relationship, but whether an increase in eccentricity corresponds to an increase in periselene or inclination — so we analyze correlation.

We will:
- Compute the correlation between eccentricity and periselene.
- Compute the correlation between eccentricity and inclination.
- Report which (if any) shows a positive correlation (i.e., when eccentricity increases, the other variable also increases).

```python
import pandas as pd

# Load the data
df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Extract relevant columns
eccentricity = df['eccentricity'].astype(float)
periselene = df['periselene (km)'].astype(float)
inclination = df['inclination (deg) (to moon equator)'].astype(float)

# Calculate correlation coefficients
corr_periselene = eccentricity.corr(periselene)
corr_inclination = eccentricity.corr(inclination)

print(f"Correlation between eccentricity and periselene: {corr_periselene:.3f}")
print(f"Correlation between eccentricity and inclination: {corr_inclination:.3f}")

# Determine if there's a positive correlation (greater than 0.3, say)
if corr_periselene > 0.3:
    print("Final Answer: periselene")
elif corr_inclination > 0.3:
    print("Final Answer: inclination")
else:
    print("Final Answer: neither")
```
