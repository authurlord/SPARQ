Step 1: The user wants to determine whether temperature T (°C) or density of air ρ (kg/m³) has a greater causal influence on the speed of sound c (m/s).  
Step 2: Since the data contains negative temperature values represented with a minus sign (e.g., "−5"), we need to ensure proper handling of these strings during parsing.  
Step 3: Load the dataframe and convert the 'Temperature T (°C)' and 'Density of air ρ (kg/m3)' columns to numeric, handling any parsing issues.  
Step 4: Calculate the correlation between temperature and speed of sound, and between density and speed of sound.  
Step 5: Compare the absolute values of the correlations to determine which variable has a stronger causal influence.  
Step 6: Print the result.

```python
import pandas as pd

# Load the table data
df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert temperature and density to numeric, coercing errors
df['Temperature T (°C)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Temperature T (°C)'], errors='coerce')
df['Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'], errors='coerce')
df['Speed of sound c (m/s)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Speed of sound c (m/s)'], errors='coerce')

# Drop rows with NaN values after conversion
df.dropna(inplace=True)

# Calculate correlation coefficients
corr_temp_c = df['Temperature T (°C)'].corr(df['Speed of sound c (m/s)'])
corr_density_c = df['Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'].corr(df['Speed of sound c (m/s)'])

# Compare absolute values of correlations
if abs(corr_temp_c) > abs(corr_density_c):
    influence = 'Temperature T (°C)'
else:
    influence = 'Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'

print(f"Final Answer: {influence}")
```
