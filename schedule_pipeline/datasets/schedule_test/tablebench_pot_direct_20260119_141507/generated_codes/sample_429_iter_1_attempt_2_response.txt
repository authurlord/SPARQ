Step 1: Analyze the table structure and content.  
The table contains physical properties of transmission lines at different frequencies (in Hz). Each row represents a frequency value and its corresponding values for resistance (r), inductance (l), conductance (g), and capacitance (c) per unit length.  

Step 2: Understand the purpose of each column:  
- `frequency (hz)`: The operating frequency of the transmission line.  
- `r (î / km)`: Resistance per kilometer (in ohms per km).  
- `l (mh / km)`: Inductance per kilometer (in millihenries per km).  
- `g (î¼s / km)`: Conductance per kilometer (in siemens per km).  
- `c (nf / km)`: Capacitance per kilometer (in nanofarads per km).  

Step 3: Identify notable trends:  
- As frequency increases, resistance (r) increases initially and then stabilizes.  
- Inductance (l) decreases with increasing frequency.  
- Conductance (g) increases with frequency (nonlinearly).
