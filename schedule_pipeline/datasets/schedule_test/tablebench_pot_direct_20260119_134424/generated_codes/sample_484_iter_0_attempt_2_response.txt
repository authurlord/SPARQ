Step 1: The user wants to identify mountain peaks with elevation and prominence that significantly deviate from the pattern. This requires analyzing the distribution of elevation and prominence values.
Step 2: Load the dataframe.
Step 3: Convert 'elevation (m)' and 'prominence (m)' columns to numeric for analysis.
Step 4: Use statistical measures (e.g., mean and standard deviation) to detect outliers.
Step 5: Identify peaks where either elevation or prominence is more than 2 standard deviations away from the mean.
Step 6: Print the names of such peaks.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Convert elevation and prominence to numeric
df['elevation (m)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['elevation (m)'])
df['prominence (m)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['prominence (m)'])

# Calculate mean and std for elevation and prominence
elevation_mean = df['elevation (m)'].mean()
elevation_std = df['elevation (m)'].std()
prominence_mean = df['prominence (m)'].mean()
prominence_std = df['prominence (m)'].std()

# Identify outliers (more than 2 std away)
outliers_elevation = df[abs(df['elevation (m)'] - elevation_mean) > 2 * elevation_std]
outliers_prominence = df[abs(df['prominence (m)'] - prominence_mean) > 2 * prominence_std]

# Combine outlier peaks
outlier_peaks = set(outliers_elevation['peak']).union(set(outliers_prominence['peak']))

print(f"Final Answer: {', '.join(outlier_peaks)}")
```
