Step 1: Identify a district with an unusually high or low population density.  
Step 2: Population density is calculated as population per km². We look for outliers — values that are extremely high or low compared to the rest.  
Step 3: Check the data for extreme values, especially in "pop density (per km2)" column.  
Step 4: The district with the highest population density (e.g., 1,000,000 per km²) is likely an outlier due to its extremely high value.  

Observing the table:  
- "San Lorenzo" has a population of 100,000 and area of 10 hectares (100,000 m² = 100 km²), so density = 100,000 / 100 = 1,000 per km². But the table shows 1,000,000 per km² — this is clearly an error.  
- All other densities are in the range of ~20,000 to 60,000 per km².  

Thus, "San Lorenzo" has an obviously incorrect data point.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Check for unusually high population density
density_values = df['pop density (per km2)']
# Find the maximum density and check if it's extremely high
max_density = density_values.max()
# Look at the district with max density
outlier_district = df.loc[density_values.idxmax(), 'district']
print(f"Final Answer: {outlier_district}")
```
