Step 1: The user wants to analyze the relationship between Peak Flow (in m³/s) and Water Level (in meters) at Trent Bridge.  
Step 2: Load the dataframe.  
Step 3: Extract the 'Level at Trent Bridge m' and 'Peak Flow m3/s' columns.  
Step 4: Since the data is not in chronological order and we are analyzing the trend, we will sort by water level (ascending) to observe how peak flow changes with increasing level.  
Step 5: Plot a scatter plot to visualize the relationship between the two variables.  
Step 6: Print the final answer as the observed trend.

```python
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert relevant columns to numeric
df['Level at Trent Bridge m'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Level at Trent Bridge m'], errors='coerce')
df['Peak Flow m3/s'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Peak Flow m3/s'].str.replace(',', ''), errors='coerce')

# Drop the row with missing rank (normal/avg flow)
df = df.dropna(subset=['Rank'])

# Sort by water level to observe trend
df_sorted = df.sort_values(by='Level at Trent Bridge m')

# Plot scatter plot
plt.figure(figsize=(10, 6))
plt.scatter(df_sorted['Level at Trent Bridge m'], df_sorted['Peak Flow m3/s'], color='blue', label='Peak Flow vs Water Level')
plt.title('Peak Flow (m³/s) vs Water Level at Trent Bridge (m)')
plt.xlabel('Water Level (m)')
plt.ylabel('Peak Flow (m³/s)')
plt.grid(True)
plt.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# Final Answer: The peak flow increases as the water level increases.
