Step 1: The user wants to identify unusual patterns or outliers in the financial data of Greek companies.  
Step 2: Load the dataframe and examine the numerical columns: revenues, profit, assets, and market value.  
Step 3: Look for extreme values (outliers) such as negative profits, very high or low values compared to others, or inconsistencies (e.g., market value much higher than assets).  
Step 4: Specifically, analyze:  
- Negative profits (losses): Identify companies with negative profit.  
- Disproportionate market value vs. assets: e.g., Piraeus Bank has a market value of 100 billion but assets of only 62.5 billion — this is highly unusual.  
- Revenue anomalies: Hellenic Telecom has revenues of 1000 billion (1 trillion), which is vastly higher than others (all below 14 billion), suggesting a possible data error or misclassification.  
Step 5: Print observations about these outliers.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert numerical columns to float, handling negative signs and '1000' as string
df['revenues (us billion)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['revenues (us billion)'], errors='coerce')
df['profit (us billion)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['profit (us billion)'], errors='coerce')
df['assets (us billion)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['assets (us billion)'], errors='coerce')
df['market value (us billion)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['market value (us billion)'], errors='coerce')

# Identify outliers
negative_profit_companies = df[df['profit (us billion)'] < 0]['company'].tolist()
high_revenue_company = df.loc[df['revenues (us billion)'].idxmax()]['company']
high_market_value_vs_assets = df[df['market value (us billion)'] > 10 * df['assets (us billion)']]['company'].tolist()

print(f"Companies with negative profit: {negative_profit_companies}")
print(f"Highest revenue company: {high_revenue_company}")
print(f"Companies with market value significantly higher than assets: {high_market_value_vs_assets}")
