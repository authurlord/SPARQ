We are asked to determine which factor—density of air (ρ) or temperature (T)—has a greater causal influence on the speed of sound (c). 

Step-by-step analysis:
1. The speed of sound in air is known to depend on temperature and density, but physically, the speed of sound is primarily a function of temperature and the adiabatic index, not directly on density.
2. However, in this dataset, we have numerical values for temperature (T), density (ρ), and speed of sound (c).
3. To assess "causal influence," we can examine how much the speed of sound changes with temperature versus density — by computing the correlation between each variable and speed of sound.
4. Since the relationship is physical, we expect temperature to have a stronger influence than density. But we will verify using correlation.

Approach:
- Compute the correlation between 'Speed of sound c (m/s)' and 'Temperature T (°C)'.
- Compute the correlation between 'Speed of sound c (m/s)' and 'Density of air ρ (kg/m³)'.
- Compare the absolute values of these correlations.

Since temperature has a strong positive effect on the speed of sound (as temperature increases, speed of sound increases), and density decreases as temperature increases (inverse relationship), we expect a stronger correlation with temperature.

Let’s compute and compare the correlations.

```python
import pandas as pd

# Load the data
df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert Temperature T (°C) and Density of air ρ (kg/m³) to numeric
df['Temperature T (°C)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Temperature T (°C)'], errors='coerce')
df['Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'], errors='coerce')

# Compute correlations
corr_temp_c = df['Speed of sound c (m/s)'].corr(df['Temperature T (°C)'])
corr_density_rho = df['Speed of sound c (m/s)'].corr(df['Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'])

# Compare absolute values
if abs(corr_temp_c) > abs(corr_density_rho):
    result = 'Temperature T (°C)'
else:
    result = 'Density of air ρ (kg/m3)'

print(f"Final Answer: {result}")
```
