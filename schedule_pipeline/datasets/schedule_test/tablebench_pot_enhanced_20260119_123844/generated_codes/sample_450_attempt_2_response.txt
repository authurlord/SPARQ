Step 1: The user wants to identify years with unusually high or low success rates compared to the overall performance.  
Step 2: Load the dataframe.  
Step 3: Extract the 'success rate' column and convert it to a numeric value (removing the '%' sign).  
Step 4: Calculate the overall success rate (from the 'total' row).  
Step 5: Compare each year's success rate to the overall rate, flagging those that are significantly higher or lower (e.g., more than 10% deviation).  
Step 6: Return the years that are outliers.  

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Convert success rate to numeric by removing '%' and converting to float
df['success rate'] = df['success rate'].str.replace('%', '').astype(float)

# Get overall success rate from the 'total' row
overall_success_rate = df[df['year'] == 'total']['success rate'].values[0]

# Define threshold for "unusually high or low" (e.g., more than 10% deviation)
threshold = 10.0

# Find years with success rate deviating more than threshold from overall
outliers = df[df['year'] != 'total']
outliers = outliers[
    (abs(outliers['success rate'] - overall_success_rate) > threshold)
]

# Extract the years
outlier_years = outliers['year'].tolist()
print(f"Final Answer: {', '.join(outlier_years)}")
```
