Step 1: The question asks to identify countries with abnormal data points compared to overall trends. Since there is no specific metric or trend defined (e.g., GDP per capita, population), we interpret "abnormal" as outliers in key indicators such as GDP per capita or GDP, especially when compared to the rest of the dataset.

Step 2: We will focus on GDP per capita as a primary indicator for identifying outliers, since it reflects economic well-being and may show extreme values (e.g., very high or very low).

Step 3: We will calculate the mean and standard deviation of GDP per capita for all countries and identify any country whose GDP per capita is more than 2 standard deviations above or below the mean — this is a common statistical method for outlier detection.

Step 4: We will also check for potential anomalies in GDP, population, or area if they seem inconsistent (e.g., extremely high GDP per capita with low population or small area).

Step 5: Filter the dataset and print the names of countries that are outliers based on GDP per capita.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert population and GDP per capita to numeric (in case of string)
df['population'] = pd.to_numeric(df['population'], errors='coerce')
df['gdp per capita (us)'] = pd.to_numeric(df['gdp per capita (us)'], errors='coerce')

# Remove rows with missing values
df = df.dropna(subset=['gdp per capita (us)'])

# Calculate mean and std of GDP per capita
mean_gdp_per_capita = df['gdp per capita (us)'].mean()
std_gdp_per_capita = df['gdp per capita (us)'].std()

# Identify outliers (more than 2 std from mean)
lower_bound = mean_gdp_per_capita - 2 * std_gdp_per_capita
upper_bound = mean_gdp_per_capita + 2 * std_gdp_per_capita

outliers = df[(df['gdp per capita (us)'] < lower_bound) | (df['gdp per capita (us)'] > upper_bound)]
outlier_countries = outliers['member countries'].tolist()

print(f"Final Answer: {', '.join(outlier_countries)}")
```
