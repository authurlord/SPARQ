We are asked to predict the expected viewership for the next episode based on the viewership trends from the listed episodes.

Step-by-step approach:
1. The data shows viewership (in millions) for a sequence of episodes.
2. We can observe the trend in viewership over time (from episode 1 to 6).
3. Since there's no clear pattern (e.g., increasing, decreasing, or cyclical), we consider computing the average viewership as a simple baseline prediction.
4. Alternatively, we might look at the trend: the viewership increases from 1.45 to 1.88 and then dips slightly. However, without more context, the safest and most reasonable assumption is to use the average viewership as a prediction for the next episode.

So, we will:
- Extract the "viewers (in millions)" column.
- Compute the mean of these values.
- Use that mean as the predicted viewership for the next episode.

Note: This is a simple extrapolation based on historical average and does not involve complex modeling due to lack of additional information.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')
# Extract viewership values and compute their average
average_viewership = df['viewers (in millions)'].mean()
print(f"Final Answer: {average_viewership:.2f}")
```
