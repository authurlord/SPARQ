Step 1: Understand the table structure and column meanings.  
- The table contains data on different engine types and their performance under various flight scenarios.  
- Columns:  
  - `engine type`: Type of engine (e.g., rocket, turbojet, turbofan).  
  - `scenario`: Operating condition (e.g., vacuum, Mach 1, cruise).  
  - `sfc in lb/(lbf h)`: Specific fuel consumption in imperial units.  
  - `sfc in g/(kn s)`: Specific fuel consumption in metric units.  
  - `specific impulse (s)`: Efficiency measure of propulsion; higher is better.  
  - `effective exhaust velocity (m/s)`: Speed at which exhaust gases are expelled; directly related to efficiency.  

Step 2: Identify notable insights:  
- Rocket engines (like SSME) have high specific impulse and exhaust velocity, indicating excellent efficiency in vacuum conditions.  
- Turbojets like J-58 and Olympus 593 perform well at high Mach numbers, with very high exhaust velocities.  
- Turbofans (like CF6-80C2B1F) have lower fuel consumption at cruise conditions, making them efficient for commercial aviation.  
- As speed increases (Mach 1 → Mach 3.2), fuel consumption drops significantly due to aerodynamic efficiency.  
- The best performance (highest specific impulse and exhaust velocity) is seen in high-speed supersonic engines.
