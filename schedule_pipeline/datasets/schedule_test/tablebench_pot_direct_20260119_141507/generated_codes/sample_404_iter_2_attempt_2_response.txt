Step 1: Understand the structure of the table. The table contains data on different engine types and their performance under various scenarios.  
Step 2: Identify and describe each column:
- "engine type": Type of engine (e.g., rocket, turbojet, turbofan).
- "scenario": Operating condition (e.g., vacuum, Mach 1, cruise).
- "sfc in lb/(lbf h)": Specific fuel consumption in imperial units.
- "sfc in g/(kn s)": Specific fuel consumption in metric units.
- "specific impulse (s)": Efficiency measure of propulsive performance.
- "effective exhaust velocity (m/s)": Velocity at which exhaust gases are expelled, directly related to efficiency.

Step 3: Extract notable insights:
- The Rolls-Royce/SNECMA Olympus 593 engine (used in Concorde) has the highest specific impulse and exhaust velocity, indicating superior efficiency at Mach 2.
- The J-58 turbojet (used in SR-71) has the highest effective exhaust velocity, suggesting exceptional performance at high speeds.
- The CF6-80C2B1F turbofan (used in Boeing 747) has the highest specific impulse and exhaust velocity, indicating it is optimized for long-haul commercial flight.
- As speed increases (e.g., Mach 3.2), fuel efficiency drops significantly, as seen in the J-58 engine with very low SFC.
- Vacuum conditions (e.g., space shuttle) have lower SFC compared to atmospheric flight, which is expected due to no atmospheric drag.
