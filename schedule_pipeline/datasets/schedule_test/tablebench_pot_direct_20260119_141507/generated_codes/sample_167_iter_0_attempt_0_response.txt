Step 1: The question asks how a change in international passengers affects the rank of an airport. This implies analyzing the relationship between "international passengers" and "rank".

Step 2: Since rank is a positional measure (lower rank = better), and international passengers are a numerical variable, we need to assess whether there's a correlation or trend between them.

Step 3: We will calculate the correlation coefficient between "international passengers" and "rank". A positive correlation would mean higher international passenger numbers are associated with higher ranks (worse position), while a negative correlation suggests the opposite.

Step 4: Use pandas to compute the correlation and interpret it.

Note: The rank is ordinal (1st, 2nd, etc.), and the international passenger count is continuous. Pearson correlation may not be ideal for ordinal data, but it can still give a general sense of trend.

```python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('table.csv')

# Convert 'international passengers' to numeric (handle any formatting issues)
df['international passengers'] = pd.to_numeric(df['international passengers'], errors='coerce')

# Drop rows with missing values
df = df.dropna(subset=['international passengers', 'rank'])

# Convert 'rank' to numeric (it's already numeric)
df['rank'] = pd.to_numeric(df['rank'], errors='coerce')

# Calculate correlation between international passengers and rank
correlation = df['international passengers'].corr(df['rank'])

print(f"Final Answer: {correlation:.3f}")
```
