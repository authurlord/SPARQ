Step 1: Understand the structure and content of the table.  
The table contains physical parameters of transmission lines or cables at different frequencies (in Hz). Each row represents a frequency level, and the columns represent electrical properties:
- frequency (Hz): the operating frequency of the transmission line.
- r (Ω/km): resistance per kilometer.
- l (mH/km): inductance per kilometer.
- g (μS/km): conductance per kilometer.
- c (nF/km): capacitance per kilometer.

Step 2: Identify notable trends:
- As frequency increases, resistance (r) increases, inductance (l) decreases slightly, conductance (g) increases significantly, and capacitance (c) remains constant.
- At lower frequencies (e.g., 1 Hz), r is low and l is high; as frequency increases to 2 MHz, r increases sharply, l decreases, and g increases due to dielectric losses.
- Capacitance (c) is constant across all frequencies, suggesting it is not frequency-dependent in this dataset.
